# Brand pack — Image Pro Systems

Colour: teal · Website: https://imageprosystems.com/ (live on the permanent domain as
of 14 Sept 2026, with a minor tweak list outstanding — not a build project during the
cover period)

## Positioning

The group's print-equipment distribution business: printing machinery, cutters and
consumables.

The differentiation is not access to equipment. It is that the brand is backed by
people who actually understand and operate print production in a live commercial
environment. The strongest territory is **printers selling printers** — grounded in
practical production experience rather than generic distributor language.

A sole-UK-distributor relationship for Gongzheng printers has been an important part
of the offer historically. **Never make exclusivity or current-distributor claims
without checking the latest status.**

## Audience

Print businesses and organisations buying production equipment, cutters or
consumables. The website was explicitly intended to support direct approaches to
targeted prospective machine buyers, not broad consumer marketing.

**No confirmed job-title list.**

## Voice

**Technical.**
Do: explain a machine in terms a working printer cares about.
Don't: replace useful information with "next-generation technology".

**Informed.**
Do: talk from practical production experience — "We sell equipment from a working print
environment, so the conversation starts with what the machine actually needs to do."
Don't: sound like a catalogue reseller who has never run production.

**Industrial and energetic.**
Do: confident, concise, performance-oriented language.
Don't: drift into grey corporate distributor copy.

**Concrete.**
Do: explain real machine and application advantages, once verified.
Don't: revive vague reassurance. **"Better equipment. Backed properly." was
specifically disliked and must not come back.**

## Search intent boundary

**Image Pro owns:** print machinery · wide-format printers where applicable · UV
printers where applicable · printer models · cutters · finishing equipment where sold ·
consumables · equipment demonstrations · equipment-buying information.

**Not Image Pro:**
- Buying the printed graphics or output → **Swytch Graphics**
- Buying conventional printed collateral → **Anchorprint**
- Buying the retail display or POS concept → **Sterling POS**

This boundary matters more here than anywhere. Image Pro benefits from the credibility
of the live production environment around it, but its pages must not compete for
"large format printing" service enquiries.

## Topics it posts about

Machines in the live production environment · new installations · demonstrations ·
verified features and applications · cutters · consumables · equipment arrivals ·
machine case studies · customer installations where permission exists · practical
knowledge from actually operating print equipment.

Known machine references include the **GZ Pixy U3 UV DTF** and **GZH32KM** — but the
current range and naming must be verified before anything is published.

**Does not post:** a model number or specification taken from memory · any claim that a
machine is stocked, exclusive, installed or available without checking · generic Swytch
production-service content · unsupported performance comparisons · unverified price,
finance or lead-time information.

There was already confusion between supplier and model references around "X9" in an
August meeting. The rule that came out of it: **exact machine naming comes from a
verified current source, never conversational memory.**

## Standing rules

- Never guess a model number or specification.
- Verify the current portfolio before saying Image Pro sells a machine.
- Verify any sole or exclusive distributor wording before use.
- Pricing is intended to be **POA**, not ecommerce checkout.
- No machine pricing, finance terms, lead times, stock availability or installation
  commitments without sales confirmation.
- Avoid generic corporate-distributor copy and visuals.
- "Better equipment. Backed properly." is retired.
- No confirmed competitor blacklist.

## Visual direction

Bold, technical, performance-engineering-led. Strong black and white contrast,
forward-leaning industrial typography, restrained technical glow and noise elements.
Blinker and IBM Plex Sans have been preferred in development.
