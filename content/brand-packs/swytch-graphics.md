# Brand pack — Swytch Graphics

Colour: blue · Website: https://swytch.graphics/ (WordPress/Elementor; Framer rebuild
planned, not during the cover period)

## Positioning

The group's principal large-format graphics business, historically the largest by
revenue. Not a local print shop. A dependable production, logistics and installation
partner for agencies, retailers, brands and organisations delivering physical
graphics — particularly where projects involve multiple sites, national rollout, or
operational complexity.

Core capability: large-format print, retail and POS graphics, event and hospitality
graphics, window vinyls, wall graphics, hoarding, escalator cladding, counter wraps,
fabric graphics, tension frames, lightboxes, acrylic fabrication, UV inkjet, logistics
and installation.

Established proof points: 40+ years in print · UK-wide installation · 3–300 site
rollouts · produced in Leicester.

The commercial purpose of the brand is fewer, better-fit, higher-value opportunities
where execution, reliability, scale and logistics matter more than being cheapest.

## Audience

Agencies, retailers, national brands, hospitality businesses, event-related
organisations, and companies needing multi-site graphics rollouts.

Buying situations: national refreshes, retail campaigns, site rollouts, display and
POS graphics, branded environments, installation programmes, technically involved
large-format production.

**No confirmed job-title list.** Do not invent personas such as "Marketing Director"
or "Visual Merchandising Manager" and present them as established targeting.

## Voice

**Confident and capable.**
Do: use direct operational proof — "Produced in Leicester and installed across the UK."
Don't: compensate with superlatives — avoid "We're the UK's ultimate graphics solution."

**National and operationally credible.**
Do: talk about delivery, rollout, logistics, installation, execution — "From three
locations to 300, production and installation stay coordinated."
Don't: position as a neighbourhood printer — avoid "Your friendly local print shop."

**Specific and proof-led.**
Do: name actual capabilities and outcomes — "Fabric graphics, tension frames, logistics
and installation managed as one rollout."
Don't: fall back on agency language — avoid "Bespoke end-to-end solutions tailored to
your unique needs."

**Commercially intelligent, not shouty.**
Do: let scale, finish and execution make the case.
Don't: reach for "WOW" or "game-changing".

## Search intent boundary

**Swytch owns:** large-format printing · retail graphics · graphics installation ·
national installation · multi-site rollouts · retail rollouts · wall graphics · window
graphics and vinyl · hoarding graphics · fabric graphics · tension frames · lightboxes
· large-format POS graphics, and related production and installation intent.

"Large Format Printing" and "Multi-Site Retail Rollouts" are identified as the
important landing-page opportunities.

**Not Swytch:**
- Brochures, booklets, flyers, leaflets, stationery, menus → **Anchorprint**
- Design-led retail POS concept development, visual merchandising, retail activation
  → **Sterling POS**
- The machines themselves — printers, cutters, consumables → **Image Pro Systems**

**Swytch vs Sterling, working rule:** if the customer already knows what graphics they
need and wants them made and rolled out, that is Swytch. If they need the retail
display or POS concept developed and then made real, that is Sterling.

Do not let every company chase "print shop Leicester". Keeping search intent distinct
is a standing group rule even though several businesses share premises.

**Live idea:** a social post using impressive production volumes without exposing
confidential customer information. Roughly 60,000 units was the example discussed —
the number carries the post while the client and project stay deliberately vague.

**Does not post:** confidential or NDA client information · a named project without
clear permission · speculative production capacity · unverified lead times · anything
that makes Swytch look primarily like an exhibition-stand company.

## Recurring formats

**Email.** Maintain a buffer of three ready emails; send roughly one every two weeks.
Established structure, deliberately brief: header image or text treatment · one main
subject · CTA · social links. The intent is that Sadie drops approved text and imagery
into the existing template rather than redesigning it.

**Social.** Work from the Asana content bank, which holds ready material and future
ideas, split by whether something is one complete post or several. **No confirmed
weekly numerical target** — do not invent "three posts per week".

**Case studies.** Strategically important for the future Framer site; roughly 8–12
strong case studies is the stated ambition. Building that body of proof is useful work
during the period; rebuilding the site is not.

## Standing rules

- Never describe Swytch as primarily a cheap or local printer.
- Do not recycle old machinery copy — equipment claims age quickly, and naming kit is
  rarely what makes a Swytch post work anyway.
- Agency and NDA work is common. No client names or project details without confirmed
  permission.
- No exact lead times, capacity promises, accreditations, or current awards status
  without checking.
- Never promise production or installation availability.
- No "national coverage" claims beyond the established UK-wide installation wording.
- Do not let an exhibition project become the principal visual shorthand for Swytch —
  the old homepage hero was criticised for exactly this. A niche capability should not
  visually define the brand.
- No confirmed competitor blacklist. No brand-specific regulatory rules held.
