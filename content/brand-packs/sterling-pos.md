# Brand pack — Sterling POS

Colour: pink · Positioning line: **"Where retail takes shape."**

## Positioning

An acquired group business being deliberately repositioned away from looking like
another production company. Sterling is the group's design-led, agency-minded retail
POS and display business: creative thinking at the front, the group's real
manufacturing, logistics and installation capability behind it.

The internal ambition is for Sterling to become the group's clearest outlet for
creativity — not a list of things it can manufacture.

## Audience

Retail-facing. Businesses commissioning point-of-sale, retail displays, visual
merchandising, and physical branded retail environments.

Buying situations: needing a retail display idea developed · concept-to-production POS
· permanent or temporary displays · a more creative retail activation · visual
merchandising projects where the solution itself needs developing.

**No confirmed job-title list.**

## Voice

**Design-led.**
Do: lead with the idea, the customer experience, or the retail problem.
Don't: open with machinery and manufacturing.

**Retail-aware.**
Do: write in the language of displays, shopper environments, physical retail.
Don't: read like a generic printer.

**Agency-minded.**
Do: show concept, design development, considered presentation.
Don't: treat creative work as a decorative add-on.

**Production-grounded.**
Do: follow the creative promise with evidence the group can actually manufacture and
install — "Concept first, then the production expertise to make it work in-store."
Don't: drift so far into agency language that the tangible production capability
disappears.

## Search intent boundary

**Sterling owns:** retail POS · point-of-sale displays · retail display design · visual
merchandising · retail display concepts · design-led retail activation.

**Not Sterling:**
- Large-format printed graphics and installation or rollout where the buyer already
  knows what needs producing → **Swytch Graphics**
- Conventional printed collateral → **Anchorprint**
- Print-production machinery → **Image Pro Systems**

**Working rule:** Sterling starts further upstream than Swytch. If the display solution
itself needs inventing or developing, Sterling. If the job is production and rollout of
already-defined large-format graphics, Swytch.

## Topics it posts about

**The current social approach is deliberately narrow: projects.**

Social functions as a **proof library** Andy can show or point customers toward — not a
follower-growth channel. When there is no new project to show, revisiting an older
worthwhile project is correct and expected.

Good subject matter: completed retail-display projects · concept-to-finished-display
stories · design development · prototypes · attractive details · strong photography of
finished work.

**How Sterling material arrives.** There is no image library to work from. **Andy sends
material directly to Sadie when he has something worth posting**, and the two of them
approve the post between them. Sterling is therefore the one context with a second pair
of eyes built in — Sadie does not need to escalate a Sterling post beyond Andy, and
should not wait on anything else.

The practical consequence: if Andy has sent nothing, there is nothing new to post. That
is a normal state, not a gap to fill with invented content. Revisit an older project or
spend the time elsewhere.

**Does not currently post:** generic engagement content · filler · "fun" social for its
own sake · broad print education · machinery posts · follower-growth gimmicks.

This can change later. For now the purpose is credibility and proof.

## Recurring formats

**Project posts** are the normal social format. No fixed quantity held.

**Brochure:** an 8–12 page brochure is part of the repositioning scope, intended to
follow or run alongside the website work. The previous brochure was criticised
internally as dated and production-led; the rule from that discussion is to rebuild
from the ground up with creativity and ideas leading and production supporting.

**No confirmed regular email series.** Previous customer communication has gone to a
database of roughly 130 customers.

## Standing rules

- Lead with creativity and design before production.
- Do not turn Sterling back into "another print company".
- Keep **"Where retail takes shape"** as the positioning line unless deliberately
  changed.
- No project or client imagery without appropriate permission.
- No pricing or quote values.
- Avoid visible third-party email-platform branding where practical.
- No confirmed competitor blacklist. No specific regulated-claim framework held.
